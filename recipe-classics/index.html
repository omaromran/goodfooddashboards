The Industry Has Tried to Solve This. None Have Cracked It — Except One.

❌ No Permanent Classics

HelloFresh Canada / Global

  • 40+ rotating recipes/week across 8 dietary categories
  • Has a "Hall of Fame" tag for beloved recipes — BUT these still rotate in and out weekly. Customers cannot access a Hall of Fame dish on demand; it appears when HelloFresh decides, not when the customer wants it
  • In the UK (HelloFresh's strongest market), they cut 15% of their workforce in late 2024, closed a major fulfilment centre putting 900 jobs at risk, and have not turned a pre-tax profit since 2020
  • Their 12-month customer retention in the UK sits at just ~9%

Verdict: Recognition exists. Access does not. Customers who want a favourite dish this week simply may not find it.

❌ No Permanent Classics

Chefs Plate (owned by HelloFresh, Canada)

  • 24 recipes/week, leaning toward simple comfort food
  • Recipes rotate every 4–5 weeks — classics do reappear, but on Chefs Plate's schedule, not the customer's
  • Budget-friendly at $8.99/serving, but no dedicated classics feature or permanent category

Verdict: Closest feel to comfort food, but still fully rotational. No guarantee.

❌ Comfort Brand Promise, No Permanent Access

EveryPlate (HelloFresh subsidiary, U.S.)

  • Built its entire brand identity around "homestyle classics and comfort food"
  • 30+ weekly recipes anchored in crowd-pleasers: pastas, tacos, chicken dishes, burgers
  • This brand-level comfort promise attracts customers — but the actual recipes still rotate weekly
  • Biggest market proof that the demand for comfort/classics is MASSIVE — yet still no always-available classics system

Verdict: They identified the need and built a brand around it. But they didn't build the feature.

Case Study ✅ SOLVED IT

GOUSTO (UK) 🏆

And the Numbers Prove It

  • Gousto formally created a permanently available category called "Everyday Favourites"
  • Their own website states: "the nation's favourite family classics, available on our menu every week"
  • How they built it: Gousto went to families across the UK, asked which meals had kids rushing to the table, identified the top 10 most beloved dishes, and made them permanently available — with reduced prep time, added veg, and a Gousto twist. These meals never leave the menu.
  • They run this alongside 75–175 new rotating recipes each week and a searchable cookbook archive of 9,000+ dishes — so customers get both comfort AND discovery, every single week
  • Additional always-on categories: 10-Min Meals, Family Classics, Joe Wicks Picks, Calorie Controlled — all permanently accessible, not rotational

Business Results (mini stat badges):

Cut losses by £55M in 2024 (from £75.6M to £20.3M) Record EBITDA £42M in 2024 — highest in company history Positive free cashflow for the first time ever Revenue +11% in H1 2025 — "market-beating growth" Expanding into Ireland to take on HelloFresh CEO: "Gousto is winning category share"

Meanwhile HelloFresh UK: closed warehouse, cut 900 jobs, no profit since 2020.

✅ Permanent classics. Named feature. Customer-led design. Proven retention results.

❌ Gap Exists — First-Mover Opportunity

Good Food (Canada)

  • 12–25 rotating recipes/week across 5 plan types: Classic, Easy Prep, Low Carb, Vegetarian, Family
  • Known for speed (10-minute meals) and culinary innovation — strong differentiators
  • But: no permanent classics section. All recipes rotate on Good Food's schedule
  • A customer who wants Butter Chicken or Classic Spaghetti this week may simply not find it — and that friction is a churn trigger

Verdict: ❌ The gap is real. And no Canadian competitor has filled it yet. Good Food can be first.

Gousto built 'Everyday Favourites' after listening to customers. Revenue grew 11% in 6 months. HelloFresh — which didn't — cut 900 jobs. The data is clear: classics aren't a nice-to-have. They're a retention engine.

Why Customers Leave — And How We Break the Cycle

1. New subscriber signs up — excited and curious
2. Explores new recipes for 2–3 weeks
3. Wants a familiar, reliable meal — can't find it
4. Doubt and friction set in
5. Cancels or discount-hops to a competitor
Good Food Classics Feature → Breaks the cycle at Step 3. Customer stays.

The Opportunity — Before & After

BEFORE

  • Rotating weekly menu only
  • Customer scrolls through unfamiliar dishes
  • No guaranteed access to favourite meals
  • Frustration builds → box skipped or subscription cancelled

AFTER

  • Two clear tabs: "New This Week 🔥" and "Our Classics ❤️"
  • Classics tab always shows: Butter Chicken, Spaghetti Bolognese, Classic Burgers, Chicken Stir Fry, Shepherd's Pie, Creamy Pasta — permanently available, never rotated out
  • Customer builds their box with any mix they want, any week
  • Discovery when they're in the mood. Comfort when they're not.
